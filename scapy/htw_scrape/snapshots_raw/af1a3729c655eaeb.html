Ask Özgün: FAQ for prospective students

FAQ for prospective students

Which degree programmes are offered at HTW Berlin?

HTW Berlin operates approximately 80 practice-oriented Bachelor’s and Master’s degree programmes. Das Spektrum umfasst Technik, Informatik, Wirtschaft, Gestaltung und Kultur. The range of subject areas offered includes technology, IT, business, design and culture. HTW Berlin also offers a number of degree programmes in English.

How are degree programmes organised? How are studies organised in general?

Study structure

  • HTW Berlin offers degree programmes leading to a Bachelor’s or Master’s degree.
  •  A Bachelor’s degree programme takes 6 or 7 semesters (3 to 3.5 years).
  • Subsequent Master’s programmes take 3 or 4 semesters (1.5 to 2 years).

Courses and examinations

  • The degree programme consists of various modules with seminars, tutorials and seminar-style courses (similar to lectures, only smaller).
  • Examinations usually take place at the end of each semester.
  • Academic achievements are measured in credit points (ECTS), whereby one semester corresponds to approximately 30 ECTS.

Practical orientation

  • As a University of Applied Sciences, our degree programmes emphasise practical relevance.
  • HTW Berlin has over 100 modern laboratories for the purposes of practical teaching.
  • All teaching staff have practical experience - a significant difference to many university employees.
  • A specialised internship is a compulsory part of the Bachelor’s degree programmes.
How do I find out which degree programme is right for me?

The study information days and a visit to the HTW Berlin Student Advisory Service can help with study orientation.

When do information events take place, e.g. the open day?

HTW Berlin offers a series of study information days, including

  • the study information days for the Bachelor’s degree programmes in June and December.
  • “Match your Master” in May.
  • the Work Exhibition for design and culture degree programmes in July.
Is a UAS degree worth less than a university degree?
  • Both degrees are formally equivalent. You can transfer to a university or higher education institution and complete a Master’s degree regardless of where you completed your Bachelor’s degree. A doctorate — currently still in cooperation with a university — is also possible at a higher education institution.
  • The difference lies in the practical orientation: Universities of Applied Sciences, formerly known as polytechnics, offer more practically-oriented degree programmes in smaller groups. Universities, on the other hand, traditionally place more emphasis on theoretical foundations and research.
  • These days, many employers place less emphasis on the type of university and more on individual qualifications, grades and practical experience. This is also shown by our excellent results in the WiWo ranking, a nationwide ranking of recruiters and HR managers.
Why should I study at HTW Berlin?

There are several compelling reasons to study at HTW Berlin:

  • Quality of teaching: HTW Berlin regularly tops academic rankings. All degree programmes are accredited, i.e. the quality of studies is regularly reviewed.
  • Practical relevance: HTW Berlin offers an attractive combination of academic research and state-of-the-art practical know-how. Courses take place in small groups of a maximum of 40 students, which ensures a personal teaching and learning atmosphere.
  • Modern equipment: HTW Berlin boasts over 100 laboratories featuring modern equipment that enable the practical application of the knowledge learnt.
  • Career opportunities: an internship integrated within every Bachelor’s degree programme and the university’s own Career Services facilitate the transition to the professional world.
  • International experience: students have the opportunity to gain experience abroad at one of our 150 partner universities. An internship abroad during your studies is also possible. In the Bachelor’s degree programme, foreign language training is an important part of the curriculum. HTW Berlin has its own Foreign Languages Centre for this purpose.
Which requirements do I need to fulfil when applying for a degree course?
  • To apply for a Bachelor’s degree programme at HTW Berlin, you need a general or subject-specific higher education entrance qualification or relevant vocational training. Some degree programmes may have additional requirements such as pre-study internships, aptitude tests or language skills.
  • A first academic degree in a specific subject is required when applying for a Master’s degree programme.
What are the application deadlines?

The application periods and the application procedure differ depending on the degree type (Bachelor’s or Master’s), higher education entrance qualification and admission requirements (aptitude test, etc.).

Can I apply before or after the application periods?

No, you cannot apply to HTW Berlin before or after the official application periods. After the regular application deadlines have expired, the website “Vacant study places” contains information regarding study places still available in individual degree programmes. 

How much does it cost to study at HTW Berlin?

At HTW Berlin, you pay a semester fee. You should also budget for an estimated 1,250 EUR per month in living expenses. Design degree programmes incur some material costs. You can reduce your study expenses by living at home and having family health insurance.

How can I find a room or apartment in Berlin?

We provide online tips on finding accommodation — from student residences to shared apartments — and on financial aid, e.g. housing benefit.

How can I finance my studies? What scholarships are available?

Various sources of funding for students exist, including jobs, BAföG, scholarships and financial support in emergencies.

We encourage students to apply for a scholarship, because for the vast majority of scholarship holders, it is no longer just grades that count. Students’ life circumstances and the conditions under which someone studies, performs and gets involved in other activities or volunteering are now of greater importance.

What extracurricular activities and sports programmes are available at HTW Berlin?

At HTW Berlin, you can get involved in many study projects and study initiatives or participate in one of the student bodies. The university sports department offers an extensive sports programme featuring numerous sports courses.

What advisory options are available for students choosing degree programmes?

The Student Advisory Service will be happy to assist you. The Study Information Days also offer a good opportunity to find out about the degree programmes on offer at HTW Berlin.

I have a health impairment, disability or chronic illness and would like to study at HTW Berlin. Who can I contact in this case?

At HTW Berlin, you can contact the Representative for Disabled and Chronically Ill Students, who will be happy to advise you. We will support you with your overall application, including special applications, help you to apply for admission in cases of hardship and advise you on compensating for disadvantages experienced during your studies.

FAQ for first-year students

When will I be enrolled?

After you have accepted the offer of a place at the university, you must submit the enrolment application attached with the PDF cover letter as well as the documents/certificates listed there and transfer the semester fee. In addition, your health insurance provider must send your health insurance certificate to HTW Berlin. After you have submitted all documents, you will be enrolled by the Student Services Department. The processing time is approx. two weeks. You will then receive an email to activate your HTW Berlin account, which unfortunately often ends up in spam.

Does HTW Berlin hold introductory events or a Freshers’ Week?

The welcome event for first-year students takes place during the first week of study. An invitation will be sent to your HTW Berlin email address.

The university also holds an enrolment ceremony for all students and specific events in your degree programme. It is wise to use the Freshers’ Week to obtain important information and establish some initial contacts among your fellow students.   

What dates and deadlines do I need to observe?

All important dates and deadlines for the semester can be found in the academic calendar.

How do I create my timetable?

At the beginning of your degree programme, you do not have to decide on your courses yourself. If you are studying full-time in the first semester, you will be automatically registered for all compulsory courses in that semester. Your degree programme contact partner will inform you of elective courses. You can view your personal timetable approximately two weeks before the start of the semester in the LSF campus management system under “My timetable”. From the second semester onwards, you are responsible for course enrolment yourself. First-year students on part-time programmes enrol on their own courses from the outset.

How do I get internet on campus?

WiFi is available for you to use at our campus locations.

How does the library work?

The University Library regularly offers training courses and guided tours — including at the beginning of the semester.

What is the best way to get to know other students?

The best thing to do is to go to the Fresher’s week events for your degree programme. Other options: Attend sports courses run by the university sports department, take part in student initiatives or join one of the student bodies.

Ask Özgün: FAQ for students

FAQ for students

Where can I find important dates and deadlines?

All important dates and deadlines for the semester can be found in the academic calendar. The time periods for course enrolment can be found in the coloured time schedule on the LSF campus management homepage.

When do I have to pay the semester fee?

The re-registration deadline can be found in the academic calendar. Please note: re-registration can only take place if no health insurance contributions are outstanding.

How do I enrol on my courses?

All important information on course enrolment, in particular on the individual phases from registration to the allocation of remaining places, has been compiled online for you.

How do I register for exams? What do I do if I am unable to attend the examination?

Information on registration and repeating examinations can be found online. The time periods for course enrolment are shown in the coloured time schedule on the LSF campus management homepage.

What do I need to consider when applying to write my thesis?

Information about the thesis, from application to publication, can be found online. Please remember: in order to apply to write the final thesis, a certain number of credit points must have been earned (see Study and Examination Regulations). For Bachelor’s degree programmes, all modules from the first three semesters must have been successfully completed.

What requirements apply to online examinations, especially with regard to permitted resources and plagiarism?

Your written examination must be an independent examination. Certain resources are permitted for online examinations. These may include personal notes, printed works (e.g. textbooks, lecture notes) or internet sources. Your lecturers will inform you exactly which resources are permitted. Please pay attention to these specifications and ask if you are unsure. The principle of good scientific practice also applies in a written examination.

If texts or key ideas are taken from documents accompanying the examination, these must be clearly labelled as quotations in order not to be considered plagiarism. To be clear: any communication with others (with the exception of the examination supervisor) and the verbatim copying of text passages (including other or jointly pre-formulated texts) without the use of inverted commas and references (“copy & paste”) are not permitted.

Please bear in mind that the principle of good scientific practice also applies in a written examination. If you take texts or key ideas from documents accompanying the examination, these must be clearly labelled as quotations or they will be considered instances of plagiarism. If violations are categorised as attempted cheating, this will result in the work being marked “unsatisfactory” (5.0), with an examination attempt deemed to have been made! In particularly serious cases of attempted cheating (e.g. repeated attempts to cheat, full plagiarism, organised collaboration between several people), this may result in your exmatriculation.

What can I do if I experience problems with grades or exams?

Examination modalities will be announced by the lecturer at the beginning of the semester. In principle, teaching staff may decide for themselves how they organise their courses and examinations. However, they must adhere to legal requirements. If you believe that decisions have not been made correctly or rules have been applied incorrectly, please refer to §18 of the Study and Examination Framework Regulations for Bachelor’s and Master’s Programmes at HTW Berlin (RStPO - Ba/Ma), “Objections to the assessment of module examinations”. You can also contact the Students' Union (AStA) for advice or support.

What to do in the event of disputed grades in examination results?

It is best to proceed as follows:

  1. Request an examination review: contact your lecturer immediately after the examination results have been announced and request an appointment to review the examination documentation. The evaluation of your examination will subsequently be explained to you.
  2. Submitting a complaint to the Examination Board: if you consider the explanation inadequate, or if you still have doubts, you can submit a written complaint to the Examination Board of your degree programme within six weeks of the announcement of the results. This complaint must be well-founded. The Examination Board will examine your objections and ask the lecturer to submit a statement. After about three weeks, you will receive a reply from the Examination Board via the examination administration department, including instructions on how to appeal.
  3. In case of organisational or content-related problems, please contact the Dean’s Office of your faculty. If your objection is rejected by the Examination Board, you can then contact the Examinations Office. The Faculty Student Councils (FSR) oder the Students' Union (AStA) are also on hand to provide support.
How do I plan my specialised internship?

Information about the specialised internship is available online. It is necessary to complete a certain number of modules before commencing the compulsory internship (see the Study and Examination Regulations).

How do I plan my studies or internship abroad?

The International Office at HTW Berlin supports you both in planning a semester abroad and in researching financing options for your internship abroad. It is advisable to begin gathering information and start the planning process between 12 and 18 months in advance.

Can I also study part-time at HTW Berlin?

Yes, HTW Berlin offers the option of part-time study.  Inform yourself in advance of any consequences of part-time study status (in terms of student health insurance, student housing, residence permit/visa, trainee student contracts, social benefits such as BAföG (educational support benefits), etc.).

What is compensation for disadvantages?

If you are unable to take module examinations in full or in part in the prescribed form, you can apply for compensation of disadvantages. The prerequisite for this is that you are not responsible for the reasons (e.g. chronic illness, caring responsibilities, disability, pregnancy and materny leave).

How does HTW Berlin support me if I enrol on a degree programme alongside caring for a child and/or caring for relatives?

You have various options. For instance, HTW Berlin offers: 

  • special regulations including preferential course enrolment, alternative examination criteria (compensation for disadvantages) as well as extended deadlines for final theses.
  • the option to record courses with the lecturers’ agreement. 
  • the option to contact the Family Support Centre, which provides childcare options (incl. emergency care).
  • the option to change to part-time studies or defer degree programmes.

Please contact the Student Advisory Service, which will be happy to advise you.

I am pregnant or breastfeeding. What should I keep in mind?

Please notify HTW Berlin of your pregnancy and observe the regulations stipulated in the Maternity Protection Act.

Who can I contact with any problems?
  • The Student Advisory Service provides answers to questions related to studies. It also advises those with difficulties as regards learning academic material or who are experiencing social problems.
  • The Psychological Counselling Service offers support in case of psychological stress - regardless of whether this is directly related to your studies (feeling overwhelmed, procrastination, exam nerves, etc.) or not (private stress such as loneliness, homesickness, bereavement, suspected depressive episode, etc.).
  • The organisation studierendenWERK Berlin offers a wide range of counselling and advisory services, including psychotherapeutic counselling, advice on financing your studies and writing guidance.
Who can I turn to in the event of discrimination at HTW Berlin?
  • The Central Anti-Discrimination Office of HTW Berlin conducts initial interviews as well as individual, group and conflict counselling for all member groups of HTW Berlin. It also acts as a Complaints Office in accordance with the General Equal Treatment Act (Allgemeines Gleichbehandlungsgesetz).
  • The Confidentiality Team at HTW Berlin is a central point of contact for those affected by and witnessing discrimination, especially sexualised discrimination and violence.

FAQ for Alumni

When does my student status end?

You will be exmatriculated as soon as you have passed the final oral examination. If you are not registered to take the final oral examination, your student status ends at the end of the previous semester.

How and when will I receive my certificate?

It takes around 12 weeks to process the final documents, as there are numerous steps that need to be completed before the certificate is finalised. As soon as the documents are ready for collection, you will be informed by e-mail. In this message, you will be asked to indicate whether you would like to collect the documents in person on site or whether you would like them to be sent by post.

When does the graduation ceremony at HTW Berlin take place?

The graduation ceremony takes place in June on the Wilhelminenhof campus. Please register to become part of our Alumni Network to receive an invitation.

How can I stay in contact with HTW Berlin?

Register with the HTW Berlin Alumni Network in order to receive invitations to HTW events, etc.

Alumni-Netzwerk

Which HTW Berlin services can I continue to use as a graduate?
  • You can take advantage of the Career Services offers for up to one year after graduation.
  • You can also book sports courses at HTW Berlin at our employee rate.
Are there opportunities for me as a graduate to share my experiences with current students?
  • You can show a student your everyday working life for a day as part of the PraxisTag (trial practical day) concept.
  • Accept a teaching assignment at HTW Berlin.
  • Some degree programmes, e.g. the Bachelor’s degree programme “Economics and Politics”, have their own alumni networks offering lecture series.
